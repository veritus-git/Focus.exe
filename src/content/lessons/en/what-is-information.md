# What is Information?

Before we dive into processors, artificial intelligence, or cryptography — we need to answer one fundamental question that connects **absolutely everything** in the digital world.

## What even IS information?

Imagine you're standing in a completely dark room. Someone flips a light switch. The light is either **on** or **off**. That's it. Two states. Two possibilities.

That single "on or off" — is a **bit**. The smallest possible piece of information in the universe.

*Right now, as you read this sentence, your screen is displaying millions of these tiny on/off signals. Every pixel, every letter, every color — all of it boils down to an astronomical number of bits being processed incredibly fast.*

---PAGE---

## Everything is numbers

Here's something that might blow your mind:

- **A photo?** Millions of pixels. Each pixel is 3 numbers (Red, Green, Blue), each between 0-255.
- **A song?** 44,100 air pressure measurements per second, each stored as a number.
- **This text?** Every letter is a number. A = 65. B = 66. Space = 32.
- **A video game?** Your position, health, inventory — all numbers being updated 60 times per second.

There's no magic. No special "image substance" or "sound material". Your computer only understands one thing: **numbers**. And at the very bottom, those numbers are made of bits — zeros and ones.

---PAGE---

## Why zeros and ones?

Not because someone decided it's cool. Because of **physics**.

A transistor — the microscopic switch inside your processor — can be in two states: **current flows** or **current doesn't flow**. On or off. 1 or 0.

Your processor has **billions** of these switches. An Apple M2 chip has 20 billion transistors on a chip the size of your fingernail.

Each one can only say "yes" or "no". But when you combine billions of yes/no answers at 5 billion times per second... you can run Minecraft.

> [!KEY] Everything you see on your screen — text, photos, videos, games — is an enormous amount of zeros and ones that your computer reads INCREDIBLY fast. That's all information is: organized bits.

## From here, everything branches out

Now that you understand what information is, you can go in **any direction**:

- **🧠 Hardware** — How does a computer physically process these bits? What are logic gates? How does a CPU actually work?
- **💻 Programming** — How do we tell the computer what to do with these numbers? What's a variable? A function?
- **🌐 Internet** — How do bits travel across the world in milliseconds?
- **📐 Math** — What patterns and structures help us organize information?
- **🤖 AI** — How can a machine learn from data?
- **🔐 Cryptography** — How do we protect information?
- **🎵 Audio** — How does a computer capture and reconstruct sound?
- **🚀 Engineering** — How do GPS, rockets, and self-driving cars use information?

**Pick a branch that excites you. There's no "right order" — only YOUR curiosity.**
