# What is Information?

Before we dive into processors, artificial intelligence, or cryptography — we need to answer one fundamental question that connects **absolutely everything** in the digital world.

## What even IS information?

Imagine you're standing in a completely dark room. Someone flips a light switch. The light is either **ON** or **OFF**. That's it. Two states. Two possibilities.

That single division into "on or off" — is a **bit**. The smallest possible piece of information in the universe.

Intuitively, information is simply the **reduction of uncertainty**. Before someone flipped the switch, you didn't know if it would be bright or dark. When the light came on, your uncertainty dropped to zero. You received exactly one bit of information.

*Right now, as you read this sentence, your screen is displaying millions of these tiny on/off signals. Every pixel, every letter, every color — it all boils down to an astronomical number of bits being processed incredibly fast.*

## Everything is numbers

Here is something that might slightly shatter your worldview:

- **A photo?** Millions of pixels. Each pixel is 3 numbers (Red, Green, Blue), each ranging from 0-255.
- **A song?** 44,100 air pressure measurements per second, stored as numbers.
- **This text?** Every letter is a number. A = 65. B = 66. Space = 32.
- **A video game?** Your position, health points, inventory — all just numbers being updated 60 times a second.

There is no magic here. Inside a computer, there is no special "image substance" or "sound material". There is only one language: **numbers**. And at the very bottom, those numbers are built of bits — zeros and ones.

Interestingly, nature does the same thing. Your DNA is also information, but instead of two states (0 and 1), it uses four (A, C, G, T).

---PAGE---

## Why zeros and ones?

It's not because zeros and ones are the most beautiful mathematical system. We use them because of the **laws of physics**.

A transistor — the microscopic switch inside your processor — has two states: **current flows** or **current doesn't flow**. On or off. 1 or 0.

Trying to build a computer that distinguishes 10 different voltage levels would end in a disaster. It would be too slow, and electrical noise would constantly cause errors. Distinguishing "there is current" from "there is no current" is trivially simple and reliable, even at billions of operations per second.

Your processor has billions of these switches. The chip inside a modern smartphone can have **tens of billions** of them crammed into an area smaller than your fingernail.

## From here, everything branches out

Now that you understand what information is, you can go in **any direction**:

- **🧠 Hardware** — How does a computer physically process these bits? What are logic gates?
- **💻 Programming** — How do we tell the computer what to do with these numbers?
- **🌐 Internet** — How do bits travel across the world in milliseconds?
- **🤖 AI** — How can a machine "learn" from these numbers?
- **🔐 Cryptography** — How do we hide information from others using math?

**Pick a path that fascinates you. There is no "wrong order" on this skill tree — there is only your curiosity.**
